The studio

A designer's plan and a stylist's eye

Most homes get one or the other. A designer draws a competent layout and leaves the room feeling unfinished; a stylist dresses a room that was never planned properly in the first place. Samiksha Warde Designs does both on the same project.

That means the wardrobe is sized around what you actually own, the lighting is planned before the ceiling closes, and someone comes back at the end to hang the art and get the shelf right. The last ten per cent is where a house starts feeling like a home.

Design with Samiksha

The process is public

Alongside client work, Samiksha publishes the thinking behind it — detail drawings for a mirror unit, why a compact TV wall is set out the way it is, what goes wrong in a kitchen that looks fine on paper.

A hundred thousand people follow that on Instagram, and more on YouTube. For a client it is a useful thing: you can watch how rooms get decided before you commit to anything.

How we work

Four things we will not trade away

01

Your budget, named early

We ask for the number at the first meeting. Knowing it lets us put the money where it is expensive to change later, and leave the rest for you to add over time.

02

Drawings you can build from

Not mood boards. Dimensioned layouts, joinery details and elevations your contractor can price and cut from without ringing us every second day.

03

Designed for how you live

We measure what you own before we draw storage — sari lengths, suitcase heights, how much hangs and how much folds. Storage is where a flat is won or lost.

04

We finish the room

The styling is part of the job, not an upsell. Lighting, textiles, art and accessories are chosen with the scheme, so the room is done on the day you move in.

Where we work

Mumbai, Thane, and your screen

On-site projects run across Mumbai and Thane — Vihang Hills, Hiranandani, the Lodha developments at Palava and Parel among them.

If you are outside that, virtual design covers the same ground over video: measured layouts, 3D views, a material list and a shopping list you can hand to a local contractor. Plenty of homes have been finished that way.
